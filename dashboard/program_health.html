Method, in one paragraph

Each run draws a weekly throughput figure at random (with replacement) from our last 4 delivery weeks [27, 5, 13, 13], subtracts it from the 90-issue backlog, and repeats until the backlog is empty. That is one run; the chart is 10,000 of them. Working weeks are mapped onto the academic calendar, so the between-semester gap and one week of fall break are not counted as productive time. Seeded, so it reproduces.

Why P50 and P85 sit only one week apart

Every run lands between Aug 31 and Nov 09, so one week carries a large share of them: P50 to P85 is one calendar week but 22 percentage points more of the runs. A week is also the smallest step the model can take. Read the tightness as a limitation, not confidence: it comes from having only 4 clean weeks to sample. Adding two ordinary slow weeks by hand moves P85 out about five weeks.

Why the headroom is not slack

It only covers ticketed work. Fall integration is not decomposed yet, so the capacity plan covers it, not this chart.

The work changes character. Summer was building on our own machines, where we are the bottleneck. Fall is integration against the client's environment, waiting on schema confirmations and sign-offs from outside the team. A bootstrap of our own throughput cannot represent that.

It assumes scope holds, and the scope line above shows it has not. So: no schedule alarm from work we can see, and buffer for what we cannot.
